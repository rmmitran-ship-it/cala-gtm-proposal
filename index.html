GTM Proposal — Prepared for Cala.ai

Where Cala wins, and how to get there.

A GTM Proposal by Raluca Mitran · July 2026

Why I wrote this.

I came to Cala's afterwork drinks and left genuinely impressed. The product has a unique place in the market and I haven't stopped thinking about the GTM motion since.

Series A is a tough step to navigate. There's a lot to test and the team gets pulled in many directions at once. I wanted to put something together to share my thinking — the goal is simple: if it resonates, I'd love to support in whatever way makes sense, whether that's external or as part of the team.

Cala has something rare: a verified knowledge layer that solves a problem most agent builders don't know they have until they're knee-deep in brittle pipelines. Early fintech traction, a working co-design model, MCP integrations live.

At Series A, that's tough to manage and hard to know what to test first. This is my take on where to start.

What follows is a diagnostic of the market, three questions I'd prioritise for H2, and a two-track growth motion — direct and partner — that a lean team can actually execute.

Where Cala is positioned uniquely.

Two ways to frame the same product — depending on who's in the room.

Outcome Driven — What You Get

OutcomeWhat It Means
EfficiencyFewer tokens consumed, no parsing overhead, faster outputs. The savings compound — the bigger the agent, the bigger the gain.
Semantic accuracyAgents reason over structured meaning, not raw text. The data has already been interpreted — so the agent spends its context on thinking, not cleaning.
VerifiabilityEvery fact is source-traced. Outputs are citable, auditable, defensible. Not "the model said so" — but "here's where it came from."

Buyer Targeted — Who You're Talking To

BuyerThe Pitch
For buildersDevelopers and technical founders care about token costs, pipeline complexity, and agent reliability. Skip the data layer entirely — query structured, pre-verified knowledge directly and spend your context on reasoning, not retrieval.
For operatorsProduct leads, analysts, and compliance teams care about whether they can trust the output. Analysis and agent decisions grounded in verified, source-traced data — not hallucinated summaries. Put your name on it.
Your agent is only as good as what it knows.

GTM Strategy

If I had a magic wand and could be supporting Cala's team for H2 — here are the questions I'd try to answer.

QuestionContextWhat Unlocks
What part of financial services should we focus on first? There is traction and provability in finance. Which sub-vertical does the product respond best to — compliance, risk, investment? How do we scale that content and expand across the competitive landscape? A repeatable sales motion built on real proof, not assumptions
Which segment do we go after next? If finance works, how do we expand smartly. Test a horizontal approach — what travels further, outcome-driven messaging (efficiency, accuracy) or purpose-driven (build better agents, get better analysis)? A GTM language that works across verticals without rebuilding the pitch every time
How do we build brand momentum? There is content waiting to be made from high-touch events and co-design sessions. Hackathons as content factories. User-generated use cases as the distribution engine. A brand that compounds without a large team behind it
Building GTM is tough. There's a lot of testing and you can lose yourself in it. That's why I always try to have a north star that anchors me.
Part 2 — Design

Which Buying Groups to Focus On.

Three tiers exist. The question is sequencing — not which to ignore, but which to win first.

SegmentACVWin FeasibilityStrategic RolePriority
Developers & startups (FinServ first) €5–20k High — traction exists Generates case studies for enterprise Lead
Enterprise CTOs (UK FinServ first) €50–150k Medium — long cycle High-value expansion from dev proof points Build toward
Solo builders (Global) <€1k High — low friction Community signal and developer advocacy Maintain

The logic: developer traction in FinServ produces the co-design case studies that open enterprise doors. Win the bottom of the funnel first — it funds the top.

Track A — Direct Motion

Two phases. The first 60 days are entirely focused on finance. Days 61–90 is a fork the market decides for you.

Days 1–60 — Go Fully Vertical

Build the Audience

  • ICP list: every retail bank, fintech, asset manager, compliance team building AI agents in UK and Spain
  • Audience mapping per account: CTO, engineering lead, compliance lead
  • Outreach infrastructure: CRM, sequences, tracking

Set Up the Motion

  • Content pillars locked: efficiency and semantic accuracy
  • Touchpoints per audience: outreach, LinkedIn, developer communities, events, partner intros
  • Test outcome-driven vs. purpose-driven messaging variants
  • Open co-design conversations with 5–10 target accounts
  • Draft first case study from existing fintech co-design
  • Sharpen /finance landing page
One goal by Day 60: finance is a machine, not an experiment.

Days 61–90 — Brand Engine vs. Horizontal Testing

Option A — Brand Engine

  • Hackathons as content factories: every build becomes a use case, a demo, a story
  • UGC flywheel: builders show up, build something real, share it, attract more builders
  • Turn users into the GTM motion

Option B — Horizontal Test

  • Don't pick a new vertical yet. Test which message travels.
  • Outcome-driven (efficiency, accuracy) vs. purpose-driven (build better agents, get better analysis)
  • Run both against Legal, Healthcare, HR
  • Whichever lands first becomes the horizontal GTM language
The signal from Day 60 makes this decision. Not today.

Track B — Partner Motion

Partners are worth planting the seed for now, even if the full motion comes later. At Series A, the right two or three partners can open doors that would take years to build directly.

Three-Way Value Framing

PartyWhat They Get
Cala getsEnterprise reach without headcount. Credibility transfer. Geographic coverage. Co-design cases become repeatable playbooks.
Partner getsA differentiated AI offering. New resell revenue stream. Stickier client relationships. First-mover advantage in their vertical before competitors embed Cala.
End client getsA battle-tested solution, not a pilot. Reduced implementation risk. Faster time to value via a partner who already knows the product.
Partners aren't a channel for today. But identifying the right two or three now means they're ready when the direct motion has proof behind it.
