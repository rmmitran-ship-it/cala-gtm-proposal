GTM Proposal — Prepared for Cala.ai

Cala doesn't need more doors open. It needs the right sequencing.

A diagnostic of where Cala stands, a framework for prioritising where to compete, and a two-track growth motion a lean team can actually execute.

Overview

The product works. The challenge is clarity.

Cala has built something genuinely rare: a verified, structured knowledge layer for AI agents that solves a problem most teams don't even know they have until they're knee-deep in brittle data pipelines and hallucinating outputs.

There is early traction — particularly in financial services and with developer teams building agentic products. The co-design model is producing real proof points.

With five industries, three buyer tiers, and a one-person GTM team, Cala is at the inflection point every Series A company reaches: the moment where doing everything means winning nothing. The window to own a category is open. But category ownership requires a decision — which door to walk through first, and how to build the engine that takes you through it.

This proposal offers a diagnostic of where Cala stands, a framework for prioritising where to compete, and a practical two-track growth motion — direct and partner — that a lean team can execute without burning runway on the wrong buyers.

It is written not to impress, but to be useful.

The Proposition

What Cala Has Built — and Why It Matters Now

The AI agent market is not short of tools. It is short of reliable inputs.

Most agent builders face the same invisible problem: their agents are only as good as the data they reason over. Web search returns noise. Scraped HTML breaks constantly. Internal RAG systems hallucinate on gaps. The developer spends 60% of their time not building the agent — but cleaning, validating, and patching the data pipeline underneath it.

Cala removes that problem entirely.

A verified entity graph — companies, people, laws, filings, market data, court records — pre-structured, source-traced, and queryable by natural language or dot-notation. An agent built on Cala doesn't hunt. It asks, and gets a typed, clean, citable answer.

Two USPs Worth Owning — Precisely Framed

Efficiency

The surface-level win. Fewer tokens consumed, less parsing overhead, faster agent outputs. This is measurable, demonstrable, and resonates immediately with CTOs managing infrastructure costs.

Semantic Precision

The deeper, more defensible advantage. Agents that reason over structured meaning — not scraped text — build better outputs, make fewer errors, and can be trusted in regulated environments. Agents don't just run faster. They reason better.

Together, these two positions occupy a lane no one else owns cleanly: the knowledge infrastructure layer for agents that need to be right, not just fast.

The core tension is not a product problem. It is a sequencing problem.

At Series A, with a lean team, trying to win Finance + Healthcare + Legal + HR + Agro across Enterprise + Developers + Solo Builders simultaneously is not ambition — it is dilution. The companies that win categories at this stage are the ones that pick a beachhead, dominate it, and use that proof to expand.

The diagnostic question is not "where could Cala play?" It is "where should Cala win first?"

Direct Motion

Always-On Digital Awareness

The goal of the always-on track is not leads. It is category education. Buyers who don't yet know they have a data quality problem need to find Cala before they know to look for it.

Three Commercial Message Pillars

Efficiency & Reliability

"Your agent is only as good as what it knows." Targets CTOs and technical founders who feel the pain of brittle data pipelines.

Build vs. Buy

"Stop building the data layer. Start building the product." Targets developers spending time on ingestion instead of the actual agent.

Trust & Compliance

"Verified facts. Not scraped guesses." Targets regulated industries where hallucination is a liability, not just an annoyance.

Landing Pages Per ICP

  • /finance — KYC, sanctions, filings, compliance agents. Already exists (good). Needs sharper headline.
  • /developers — Token efficiency, MCP integration, docs-first experience. Does not yet exist as a distinct page.
  • /enterprise — Governance, traceability, co-design model. Does not yet exist.

Developer Hooks (Always-On, Low Cost)

  • Publish in LangChain, LlamaIndex, and n8n community channels
  • "Build with Cala" tutorial content — one use case per vertical, written for the developer, not the buyer
  • MCP integration visibility — make Cala the default recommended knowledge layer in Claude and Cursor communities
  • GitHub presence — open examples, quick-start repos, low friction to first API call

High-Touch Precision

1:1 — Top Accounts

Target: 5–10 financial services firms actively building AI agents.
Approach: lead with the problem, not the product.
Offer: co-design engagement.
Output: a live case study and a paying client.

1:Few — Events & Roundtables

Target developer meetups in London and Madrid. Host a roundtable: "The AI Agent Data Problem — Why Your Pipeline Is Lying to You." Partner with fintech accelerators (Wayra, Anthemis, Level39).

Partner Motion

Partners are not a distribution channel. They are a force multiplier on a GTM motion Cala has already chosen. Cala cannot hire its way into enterprise accounts at Series A. But partners already have the trust, the relationships, and the vertical expertise.

Territory Penetration Model

UK — Financial Services

~2,000 regulated firms → ~400 AI-active (20%) → ~80 with a live data problem. Via 2 UK fintech partners, 15% conversion: 12 clients in Year 1 — 3% of the addressable base.

Spain — FinServ + Agro

Home market advantage, lower CAC, less competition. 2 Spanish partners (fintech + agro): 6–8 clients in Year 1. Strategic value: first published case studies, proof point for US expansion.

US — Financial Services

Largest TAM, highest competition and ACV. Do not enter direct in Year 1 without a strategic partner already in market: 2–3 enterprise clients via partner, beachhead not volume.

DACH / Nordics — Year 2

High compliance culture, strong fit for the verified data angle, low competition from specialists. Target: 1 regional partner in Month 10–12 as Year 2 setup.

How the Two Tracks Reinforce Each Other

  • Direct motion builds brand and inbound — developers find Cala, use it, talk about it
  • Partner motion accelerates enterprise conversion — partners bring credibility into accounts Cala can't cold-open
  • Co-design cases feed both tracks — a fintech case study is a developer tutorial AND an enterprise sales asset AND a partner enablement tool
  • Developer adoption creates pull for enterprise — when the CTO's engineering team is already using Cala, the enterprise sale becomes an expansion, not an acquisition
Two engines running in parallel, each making the other more efficient over time.
